How to read this

Every listing here is real and was publicly advertised. Nothing was invented. The research environment blocked direct page fetches, so listing detail was harvested from search-engine result content rather than by opening each page. Treat every price as last advertised, not confirmed live today, and re-verify with the agent before acting.

Links vary in directness. Ten properties link straight to their own listing page. Seven link to an agency index where the property is one entry among several. Eleven link to a portal's search results, because no per-property URL was reachable, and portal results rotate over time. Every card labels its link Listing, Agency page or Search page, and the detail panel says what to look for once you get there.

Coordinates are approximate. No listing published exact coordinates, so each pin sits on the named town, valley or sector. Use it to judge the region, never the parcel boundary.

Unknown means unknown. Fields the listing did not state are marked Unknown rather than filled with a guess. Where a dimension could not be scored, a neutral 5 stands in for the overall figure and the card flags it. Observations the listing stated are tagged verified; anything inferred from regional geography or statistics is tagged assumed.

Safety scores describe areas, not parcels. Ecuador's homicide rate hit a record 50.9 per 100,000 in 2025 and then fell around 11% through 2026, but the violence is concentrated: Guayas, Manabí, El Oro, Los Ríos and Esmeraldas account for roughly 88% of murders, while Azuay, Loja, Imbabura, Cañar, Tungurahua and Napo sit far below the national average. A favourable provincial statistic does not make any specific property safe.

Before southern Loja or Zamora-Chinchipe: foreigners hold the same ownership rights as Ecuadorians, but land within 50 km of the Peruvian or Colombian border sits in a military security zone requiring special permission. Check that line first for anything near Yangana or further south.
